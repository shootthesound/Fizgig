# Fizgig v9.7.8: download a dataset onto a pod, caption jobs in the queue, and every preview setting in one place

**Download a training folder straight onto a pod.** A big dataset no longer has to go up from your home connection. On a pod, the Start tab has a new card, **Download a training folder to this pod**: paste a Hugging Face dataset link (the whole dataset or one of its folders; a private one needs the pod's HF_TOKEN), a Google Drive share link to a zip, or a direct link to a zip or tar, and press **Download**. It checks the pod's free disk first and says plainly when the dataset won't fit, shows how much has arrived, the speed and the time left, unpacks an archive, and carries on where it stopped if you press Download again after a Stop or a dropped connection. **Train on this folder** then makes it the training folder. Sub-folders are kept as they are: pick one with **Choose…**, or tick **Also train on its sub-folders** to train them all. A Google Drive folder can't be downloaded in one go, so zip it first. The pod guide has the details (#308, thanks @girzai).

**Caption jobs in the queue.** The Captions tab has **Add to queue** beside Caption All: it lines up an AI caption job for the training folder with the tab's settings, in the same queue as your runs. Queue the captions, then queue the run that trains on them, and the run waits until they're written. The job starts at once if nothing is training. Stop holds the queue, as it does for a run. A caption job shows in the queue as **AI captions** with its folder, and in the job pill like a queued run: pick it (or **Edit** on its queue row) and the Captions tab shows its folder and settings, which you can change and keep with **Save to queue**. Your own Captions tab settings are kept aside meanwhile and come back when you switch away.

**Every setting your previews use.** Under the Samples gallery's preview line, **Every setting these previews use** lists what the previews render with: the model, this run's LoRA and its strength (and whether it's the EMA weights), sampler, scheduler, steps, CFG, the negative prompt, the size and the seeds. **Copy** puts the list on the clipboard, so you can match a preview in ComfyUI (#330, thanks @Kaoru8).

**Automagic v4: how many weights it's slowing.** On an Automagic v4 run, the run card's Learning section has a **Weights braked** cell: the share of weights the optimizer is slowing right now because they're more sensitive than its reference. The step bar shows it as `brk=`, and each epoch's line in the log gives the share with each group's sensitivity reference (#349, thanks @CognitiveDiffusion).

**Your computer's own file window.** On the computer running Fizgig, every **Choose…** now opens your system's own Open or Choose folder window, starting in the folder already in the box. On a pod, a phone or another computer, Fizgig's own picker works as before. Thanks @Murf-Code, from his Fizgig Advanced.

**Krea 2 previews on 16 and 24 GB AMD cards.** Krea 2 in Studio and the Profiler could run out of memory or stop its worker at high resolutions on ROCm. On AMD cards of 24 GB or less, Krea 2 now plans which blocks stay on the card for the size being rendered, encodes the text, samples and decodes the picture in separate stages, and moves the model to system memory between renders. On Linux it can also keep a stored fp8 checkpoint's weights in the file instead of copying them into RAM. NVIDIA cards and big AMD cards are unchanged. On an RX 9070 XT, Studio rendered at 1024 and 2048 without failures (#363, thanks @xGnome).

**AMD: no expandable memory segments on Radeon cards.** On consumer Radeon cards, PyTorch's expandable memory segments silently corrupt memory on current ROCm: garbage pictures, a model weight turning to NaN, and in larger tests corruption of the desktop itself. Fizgig's AMD launchers turned them on. They now stay off on Radeon cards, on any card Fizgig can't identify, and in the Windows AMD launcher, and stay on only for AMD's datacenter cards (Instinct MI100, MI200, MI300, MI350), where they help memory fit. `FIZGIG_NO_EXPANDABLE=1` still turns them off everywhere. One trade-off: a run on a 16 GB Radeon that only just fitted with them can now run short once its full-size pictures start. Fizgig warns at the start of such a run, on Linux as on Windows, and says what to turn off: Weight averaging (EMA), Target Megapixels, or LoKR (found by @xGnome in #363).

**Fixes.**
- **MiniMax H3 previews and Studio clips with sound keep their last frame.** A 56-frame clip came out as 55, which H3 then refuses as a training clip.
- **New caption lines fill in while captioning.** With **Add as another line**, a caption that gained a line didn't show it on the Captions tab until the job ended.
- **Long folder names in the queue.** A queued item with a long folder path squeezed its name into a column one letter wide.

**To update:** run the updater as usual.
